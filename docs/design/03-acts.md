# Acts and pacing

Source: spec §6, §17. This is the narrative frame the slice must fit into, not a script.

## 1. Act structure

| Act | Promise | Content focus | Slice? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue — The Merchant | Learn the loop; one unexplained moment | Ugarit, market, first cast, first recognition | **Yes** |
| Act I — The Connected World | The world works; reputation and language open doors | Eastern Mediterranean trade, factions, rumours of distance | Partial |
| Act II — Rumours and Fractures | The world is straining | Shortages, displaced people, risky routes, choices with costs | No |
| Act III — The Breaking of the Old Networks | Familiar places change or vanish | Destructions, rescues, record preservation, loss | No |
| Act IV — New Roads | People rebuild; the map redraws | Refugee networks, new communities, South and East | No |
| Act V — The Long Memory | The protagonist's continuity is undeniable | Descendants, inherited stories, old choices returning | No |
| Endgame — The Roads That Remain | Meaning, not victory | Combined ending families, optional continued travel | No |

## 2. Prologue and slice content

The slice is the Prologue plus the first step of Act I. It must establish:

- The protagonist is a merchant, not a hero.
- Travel is the engine of discovery.
- People have lives that continue without the player.
- Language and relationships change what is possible.
- History is present but not yet catastrophic.
- One thing happened that the protagonist cannot explain.

The slice ends on return to Ugarit (or arrival back in Cyprus), with the loop closed and
a reason to continue: a letter, a rumour, a name, a person who needs help.

## 3. Pacing rules

- Not every journey is a crisis. Aim for roughly one authored complication per 2–3
  journeys, and at least one quiet vignette per journey.
- Allow the player to stay. Time in a place should produce relationships and language,
  not just waiting costs.
- Alternate scale: a market argument, then a palace audience, then a night on the water.
- Delayed consequences are the default for meaningful choices. Immediate feedback is for
  small things.
- The historical timeline advances through authored triggers, not a visible countdown.
- Never block the main narrative behind a single skill check, language, or faction.

## 4. Emotional range checklist

Every act should contain scenes that produce at least four of:

wonder · warmth · competence · tension · grief · hope · mystery · quiet observation.

The Bronze Age Collapse is the backdrop, not the whole tone. Ordinary life, humour,
craft, food, music, and hospitality carry equal weight.

## 5. Endgame logic

- Endings are composed from flags and state, not chosen from a menu.
- Regional endings vary: a Mediterranean-focused run ends differently from one that
  reached Nubia, South Asia, or Anyang.
- No region is mandatory for every ending.
- The protagonist's final choice is about what their existence means: remember, preserve,
  protect, belong, move on, accept or refuse the divine name, or be forgotten.
- Post-ending play reflects the ending; it does not pretend the story reset.

## 6. Tone guardrails

- No grimdark nihilism. Suffering is specific and human, not decorative.
- No triumphalism. The player does not conquer history.
- No exoticism. Every culture is presented from inside its own logic.
- No modern moral framing imposed on ancient institutions; characters disagree in
  period terms.
- No fake quotations. Invented speech is clearly authored drama, never "the tablets say".
- Mystery is maintained by restraint, not by withholding basic information.

## 7. What the slice must prove

From spec §22.1, restated as testable questions:

1. Is the travel loop enjoyable on its own?
2. Does trade give a reason to move?
3. Do characters make the player want to return?
4. Is language progression rewarding rather than obstructive?
5. Do faction relationships create understandable opportunities?
6. Does the world feel vivid and specific?
7. Does the mystery create curiosity without confusion?

M4 records evidence for each, with playthrough notes.
