# Slice cast — briefs

Nine people, all invented, in a documented world. Eight of them follow one rule: they
want something that is not the protagonist, and they can be understood without the
protagonist's mystery. Belet is the deliberate exception: her craft, fear and first want
stand on their own, but her second want belongs to the recognition thread. She offers a
resemblance and a rumor, never an identity, and she is not a quest-giver. Written to the
template in `docs/templates/character.md`.

Ages are briefs for voice and casting, not stored data: see
`docs/design/04-demography.md` §5 for each one's age and the population shape they sit in.

## Amnit — merchant, Ugarit (`char.ugarit.amnit`)

- **Wants:** a contract that survives the winter; her household's name kept clean.
- **Fears:** a palace requisition; being thought a bad creditor.
- **Contradiction:** distrusts officials, needs their permission.
- **Voice:** short sentences, prices before pleasantries, dry humor about boats.
- **Limits:** she never explains the protagonist to themselves. She is not a quest-giver;
  she is a colleague who may become a friend.
- **Recurring:** appears at Ugarit and, later, on the island. She is a colleague, not
  a slice arc; her multi-act arc is the M6 worked example (`NARRATIVE_SYSTEMS.md` §13)
  and M6-T10 ports her into the arc runtime.

## Hannu — harbor official (`char.ugarit.hannu`)

- **Wants:** the dues collected; no incident he has to explain upward.
- **Fears:** being blamed for someone else's cargo.
- **Contradiction:** enforces rules he privately thinks are foolish.
- **Voice:** procedural, weary, occasionally precise about what he did not see.
- **Limits:** his favor is institutional, not personal. He will not risk his post.

## Yada — scribe and teacher (`char.ugarit.yada`)

- **Wants:** a copy of a text he has only heard of; a student who does not waste his morning.
- **Fears:** the archive being moved without him.
- **Contradiction:** teaches for silver and resents being paid for it.
- **Voice:** corrections, small vanities, sudden generosity he immediately covers.
- **Limits:** language is his trade, not his religion. He does not mystify writing.

## Tiro — shipmaster (`char.ugarit.tiro`)

- **Wants:** a full hold and an early tide; a favor owed by someone useful.
- **Fears:** a winter crossing with a tired crew.
- **Contradiction:** complains about passengers, cannot afford to lose them.
- **Voice:** imperatives, weather, and a refusal to explain twice.
- **Limits:** the crossing is work. He is not an oracle and does not know the plot.

## Belet — temple singer (`char.ugarit.belet`)

- **Wants:** the feast sung properly; to know why a name will not leave her alone.
- **Fears:** being right about something she cannot prove.
- **Contradiction:** trusts her craft, distrusts her conclusions.
- **Voice:** liturgical cadence in quotation, plain speech otherwise; asks rather than
  declares.
- **Limits:** she offers a resemblance and a rumor, never an identity. She does not know
  where the hymn came from beyond the trader who taught it.
- **The exception:** her second want — the name and the hymn — is the one cast thread tied
  to the protagonist's mystery. Her craft, fear and first want stand without it; she still
  does not explain the protagonist. This is deliberately unresolved in the slice; the
  mystery ladder in `docs/design/02-protagonist.md` §6 caps what may be confirmed, and
  M6-T07 owns recognition rules.

## Sibtu — weaver, displaced household (`char.ugarit.sibtu`)

- **Wants:** work for three more pairs of hands; not to be a burden on her cousin.
- **Fears:** being sent back inland; the children forgetting their father's house.
- **Contradiction:** asks for help and resents needing it.
- **Voice:** practical, unsentimental about her own suffering, precise about cloth.
- **Limits:** her thread is about obligation between ordinary people, not the protagonist's
  nature. Helping or refusing both have consequences that outlive the scene.
- **The slice arc:** her cloth thread is the one complete character arc authored inside
  the two destinations — ask, wait, carry, sell, then pay or keep. Both endings are
  recorded in the journal; see `docs/design/slice-arc-audit.md`.

## Kinyras — copper broker, Cyprus (`char.cyprus.kinyras`)

- **Wants:** a buyer who will not argue about weight; his name in a mainland ledger.
- **Fears:** a short shipment; being cut out of the mainland trade.
- **Contradiction:** bargains hard and keeps his word exactly.
- **Voice:** numbers, conditions, and a stated preference for unhappy settlements.
- **Limits:** he is not a villain and not a friend. He is a counterparty.

## Ada — smith, Cyprus (`char.cyprus.ada`)

- **Wants:** tin enough to finish the season; her daughter to learn the trade properly.
- **Fears:** a workshop with no metal.
- **Contradiction:** proud of work she thinks nobody sees.
- **Voice:** technical, brief, and unexpectedly funny about customers.
- **Limits:** she explains her craft, never the island's politics.
- **The workshop:** she is the smith and owns it. Her daughter, about nine, sorts
  charcoal. A woman heading a bronze workshop is unusual in the surviving evidence but not
  excluded, so she is written as a plausible local case rather than an exception the story
  comments on — and no man stands at her anvil, in prose or in art.

## Shipti — innkeeper, Cyprus (`char.cyprus.shipti`)

- **Wants:** travelers who pay on time; news from the mainland.
- **Fears:** a quiet season.
- **Contradiction:** pretends not to listen, remembers everything.
- **Voice:** questions disguised as statements; gossip delivered as caution.
- **Limits:** her information is hearsay and priced accordingly. She is the slice's rumor
  channel, not its narrator.
