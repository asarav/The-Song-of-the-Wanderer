# Dossier — Cyprus

**Status:** slice destination, authored · **Confidence basis:** copper production, harbor
settlements, and the name Alashiya are attested; the settlement, its people, and its prices
are a composite invented for the slice.
**Time:** c. 1230 BCE.

## Why this place

Cyprus is where the eastern Mediterranean's copper comes from, and therefore where a
traveler's cargo changes kind: grain and cloth go out, ingots come back. It is also the
place where a Levantine merchant stops being able to read the room — different speech,
different script, different obligations.

## The island in this period

- **Not one kingdom.** The island held several polities and many settlements. The slice
  shows one harbor community, not a state.
- **Copper is the export.** Oxhide ingots are the signature shape; smiths work bronze with
  imported tin. Workshops are household-scale and open to the yard.
- **Harbors face both ways.** Aegean, Anatolian, and Levantine traffic meet here. That is
  why the slice can put an Aegean tablet-name and a Levantine merchant in the same yard.

## Alashiya

The name appears in the letters of kings as a source of copper and a kingdom worth writing
to. The standard reading identifies Alashiya with Cyprus (or part of it); it is a reading,
not a certainty, and the game says so. The islanders in the slice do not call their home
Alashiya — that is what other people's scribes call it.

## Language and writing

- A local speech the traveler hears and cannot place. Its writing (Cypro-Minoan) is
  attested and **undeciphered**. The game never translates it and never pretends to.
- Exposure produces familiarity, not fluency: a handful of words, a gesture, the sense of
  a joke going past.
- **Akkadian** is the shared trade tongue with mainland merchants; some brokers speak it
  well and use it as a weapon.

## The harbor as the slice uses it

- **The copper yard** — ingots by weight, a broker who buys at the yard price and sells at
  whatever the boats say.
- **The landing** — boats drawn up on shingle, nets mended in shade, passage home to arrange.
- **The smithy** — a workshop open on one side, Ada at the forge, her daughter sorting
  charcoal, a tripod being made for a man who will never use it.
- **The inn** — two rooms, a courtyard, an innkeeper who remembers everything and pretends
  not to listen.

## Names

The tablet spelling **e-ma-a₂** appears on a jar label the traveler is shown: a name in a
script from across the sea, older than the person wearing it, or a coincidence. The scene
keeps both readings. See ADR-0006.

The spelling is documented in the Linear B tablets (PY Tn 316, verso line 7; the compound
e-ma-a₂-o appears on KN D 411). The reading as the later Hermes is **strongly-supported**:
the dossier and codex may say "read as", the game never asserts it.

## People (all invented)

Kinyras (copper broker), Ada (smith), Shipti (innkeeper). Roles are plausible; biographies
are fiction. Briefs in `design/characters/slice-cast.md`.

Ada runs the workshop herself. A woman heading a bronze workshop is unusual in the
surviving evidence but not excluded, so the slice presents it as a plausible local case and
the picture follows the prose: she is at the forge, her daughter sorts charcoal.

## What the slice deliberately avoids

- No "Sea Peoples" arrival, no collapse, no invasion narrative.
- No claim that Cyprus was unified, Greek, or Phoenician in this period.
- No translation of Cypro-Minoan, and no invented decipherment.
- No exotic-island atmosphere: the yard is a workplace.
