## Story of the session

I opened as a trader with 40 silver and two jars of oil, asked the porter what oil fetched, paid the three-shekel gate tax, and went up to the market. I bought two grain for the crossing and three wool cloth to sell on the island, which left five shekels. Tiro quoted eight for the coastal passage, so I worked a day on the quay to make the fare. The crossing produced a quiet anchorage, a patrol boat, and a toll I could not pay; I worked a day aboard instead. On Cyprus I found the arrival conversation with Shipti before the island had been described, then sold the cloth at a loss and the oil at a profit. Kinyras bought the remaining grain for twenty-four shekels, and two days later Shipti gave me the sealed letter from Hannu. The journal recorded the crossing, the copper agreement and the letter, but the successful counter-offer branch did not record the deal at all.

- Severity: major

- Steps:
  1. Begin a new run and travel from Ugarit to Cyprus.
  2. Finish the crossing encounters.
  3. Read the arrival scene, then the destination introduction.

### B2 — A successful counter-offer records no copper deal

- Severity: major
- Where: Kinyras, Cypriot copper yard, after the scheduled offer

  2. Spend the day to receive the offer.
  3. Choose “Thirty-six, or I carry it to the yard myself.”

The crossing has real texture: the anchorage, the patrol, the toll, and the choice to work all made the sea feel like a place rather than a loading screen. Trading itself is legible, but the first route only teaches “buy low here, sell high there” through a loss on cloth and a gain on oil; the language and faction systems were not legible enough in one run to judge their reward. The world is the strongest part: Ugarit's gate, the market scale, the Cypriot yard and the copper yard all feel specific. The letter is a good closing hook, but the counter-offer bug meant the run's own record did not support it until fixed.

  behind Shipti's line. The earlier version repeated Shipti's portrait as both the hero and
  the scene figure; the doorway now has three distinct plates, and the room node has its own

- The room cost line reads **Costs 3 shekels · 1 day** and taking it moved the date from day 6

  normal.

  followed by **A letter with a seal**; the scenario also asserts the deed and merchant trust.

## Recommendation

Fix the arrival order and the counter-offer record first; both break the story's causal chain. Then make the harbor instruction name Tiro so the first crossing is discoverable without hunting.
