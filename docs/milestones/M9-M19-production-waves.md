# M9–M19 — Production waves

Directional plan for everything after Gate B (M8). These milestones are specified here
and split into individual files at the start of each wave, so late content is planned
with the knowledge gained earlier.

**Rule:** do not author region content before its dossiers exist and its phase table is
approved. Do not start a region milestone until the previous one passes Gate C.

## Common deliverables per region milestone (M9–M14)

Every region milestone delivers its **full chronology**, not just an opening state:

1. **Pack** — manifest, dependencies, region and destination entities.
2. **Dossiers** — one per destination, ten sections, evidence ledger, bibliography.
3. **Phase table** — start state through the region's end state, with dates.
4. **World-event hooks** — what enters the region, when, and how it is felt locally.
5. **Destinations** — 4–6 per region; activities for every phase they exist in.
6. **Cast** — 4–6 major, 10–15 recurring, 20–40 local; briefs and itineraries.
7. **Arcs** — one per major character, with resolution and failure states.
8. **Languages** — family, script, tiers, learning path, interpreters, mutual
   intelligibility.
9. **Factions** — institutions, interests, obligations, conflicts, control.
10. **Economy** — regional goods, production/consumption, price bands, contracts.
11. **Routes** — regional network plus joints to neighbouring regions; requirements.
12. **Storylets** — arrival, market, road/harbour, temple, evening pools.
13. **Knowledge plan** — what can be learned, from whom, how it distorts.
14. **Scenarios and coverage** — one scenario per major arc; coverage report clean.
15. **Historical review** — provenance and reviewer recorded.

### Content budget per region

| Item | Budget |
|---|---|
| Destinations | 4–6 |
| Major characters | 4–6 |
| Recurring characters | 10–15 |
| Local characters | 20–40 |
| Events and storylets | 80–150 |
| Dialogue | 15,000–25,000 words |
| Journal/codex | 4,000–8,000 words |

Exceeding the budget is allowed only with a written reason in the milestone file and a
corresponding cut elsewhere.

## M9 — Act I: The Connected World (Eastern Mediterranean)

**Goal:** make the opening region a real act: a functioning network with visible
tensions, before the collapse.

**Destinations (candidates, to be researched):** Mycenae, Pylos or Tiryns, Knossos or
Chania, Hattusa, Ugarit (expanded inland ties), Byblos, a Canaanite inland town, Enkomi
or Kition on Cyprus, a Rhodes or coastal Anatolian stop.

**Languages:** Mycenaean Greek (Linear B), Hittite, Ugaritic, Akkadian, Canaanite
dialects, Hurrian where appropriate.

**Factions:** palace administrations, temple institutions, merchant houses, Hittite
court, harbour authorities, craft communities.

**Economy:** copper, tin, bronze objects, textiles, olive oil, wine, grain, timber,
silver, pottery, aromatics.

**Routes:** Aegean sea lanes, Anatolian overland, Levantine coast, Cyprus crossroads.

**Act I threads:** reputation across courts; a merchant network that spans three ports; a
craft family displaced by palace demand; first rumours of routes east and south; the
protagonist's local names begin to diverge.

**Exit (Gate C):** 8–10 destinations, two language families, one regional arc with three
outcomes, a full travel chain from Ugarit to the Aegean, and a persona run that completes
Act I without the main mystery.

## M10 — Egypt and the Nile

**Goal:** a major region with multiple destinations and layered access to power.

**Destinations:** Memphis, Thebes, a Delta capital/port, Elephantine or a southern
frontier town, one agricultural Nile settlement.

**Languages:** Late Egyptian; Akkadian for diplomacy; frontier languages.

**Factions:** royal court, temple of Amun, army, provincial governors, frontier garrison,
merchant households.

**Economy:** grain, gold, linen, incense, ivory, horses, chariots, craft goods.

**Unique systems:** river travel with schedules and winds; court audience earned through
relationships, service, language, and political judgement; temple festival cycles;
tribute and taxation as narrative pressure.

**Arcs:** a scribe family split between court and province; a soldier returning from the
frontier; a priestess who recognises a name; a Nubian intermediary navigating two worlds.

**Exit:** five destinations, court access without a single approval meter, Nile travel
distinct from sea travel, and at least one Egyptian character who reappears in Nubia.

## M11 — Nubia, the Red Sea, and Punt

**Goal:** the southern expansion, with river, desert, and maritime corridors.

**Destinations:** an Egyptian-administered Kushite centre (e.g. Amara West or Soleb),
a Middle Nile trading settlement, a desert road stop, a Red Sea coastal port (composite
where evidence is thin), and a Punt-associated coastal destination that stays
geographically unresolved.

**Languages:** Egyptian as administrative lingua franca; local Nile languages researched
per community; Red Sea trade communication.

**Factions:** Egyptian administration, local elites, pastoral communities, river traders,
Red Sea expedition organizers.

**Economy:** gold, ivory, ebony, incense, resins, livestock, exotic animals, craft goods.

**Hard rules:** distinguish Kerma-period history from later Kushite states; do not
backdate Napata or Meroe; do not fix Punt's location; do not assign modern identities.

**Unique systems:** expedition logistics and provisioning; river cataracts; desert
crossings; tribute and gift exchange; uncertainty in maps and rumour.

**Exit:** four to five destinations, Punt playable but unresolved, a migration chain from
the Mediterranean to the Nile, and no false certainty in codex or dialogue.

## M12 — Mesopotamia and the Iranian plateau

**Goal:** urban commercial and scribal worlds, and the overland bridge east.

**Destinations:** Babylon, Assur, a temple city (e.g. Nippur), Susa, a Zagros caravan town,
a Central Asian oasis or corridor settlement.

**Languages:** Akkadian, Elamite, local Iranian and Central Asian languages, Aramaic only
where the chronology allows.

**Factions:** Kassite court, Assyrian court, temple households, merchant families, Elamite
court, caravan confederations.

**Economy:** textiles, metals, tin, lapis, grain, dates, horses, craft goods.

**Unique systems:** temple and palace archives as knowledge sources; caravan contracts;
diplomatic letters; the changing balance between regional powers.

**Exit:** trade chain from the Levant to Central Asia with intermediaries, no direct
Levant–China route, and two competing political centres the player must navigate.

## M13 — South Asia

**Goal:** a fully playable, diverse subcontinent — not a single kingdom.

**Destinations:** a post-urban Indus settlement, a pastoral camp, a northwest trade town,
a Ganges/Yamuna settlement with Painted Grey Ware, a river crossing or pilgrimage site.

**Languages:** Old Indo-Aryan for ritual and some elite contexts; regional languages;
trade communication. Do not treat later Sanskrit literature as a transparent record of
everyday speech.

**Factions:** local lineages and elders, pastoral communities, ritual specialists, craft
and trade groups. Avoid projecting later states or a unified empire.

**Economy:** cattle, horses, grain, textiles, beads, metals, forest products.

**Unique systems:** oral tradition as knowledge transmission; guest hospitality and
lineage obligation; ritual participation; monsoon and river seasonality.

**Hard rules:** the Mature Harappan urban system is already in decline; do not equate
material cultures with textual identities; do not present Vedic traditions as fixed canon.

**Exit:** four to five destinations with distinct ecologies, at least one story heard in
two variants, and no unified political frame imposed on the region.

## M14 — China and the Late Shang world

**Goal:** Anyang and the Shang–Zhou transition, through local perspectives.

**Destinations:** Anyang/Yinxu, a royal frontier garrison, a bronze-production settlement,
a Zhou-allied or neighbouring polity, an oasis/intermediary stop on the approach.

**Languages:** Old Chinese; oracle-bone script as a scribal/ritual register; interpreters.

**Factions:** Shang royal court, royal lineage, diviners, bronze workshops, frontier
commanders, Zhou coalition.

**Economy:** bronze, jade, horses, millet, grain, silk, shells, captives (handled with
historical care and no gratuitous violence).

**Unique systems:** divination as an authored knowledge source; ritual and court politics;
bronze gift exchange; warfare as backdrop and consequence, never as the core loop.

**Hard rules:** distinguish contemporary Shang evidence from later Zhou accounts; the
transition is not a morality tale; do not let the protagonist cause the conquest.

**Exit:** four destinations, the transition experienced through at least three local
perspectives, and a route chain of intermediaries rather than a direct Mediterranean–
China passage.

## M15 — The Breaking (Acts II–III)

**Goal:** the collapse as authored, regionally varied waves that the player experiences
through people, not exposition.

**Window:** c. 1210–1150 BCE, with regional offsets.

**Deliverables:**

- World-event waves per region: Mediterranean destructions, Hattusa's abandonment,
  Ugarit's fall, Mycenaean palace failure, Egypt's survival and exhaustion, Cyprus
  reorientation, Mesopotamian contraction, Nubia/Red Sea comparatively insulated.
- Route closures and risky states with authored reasons.
- Destination states: destroyed, abandoned, occupied, refugee-filled, weakened.
- Migration groups with origins, routes, temporary refuges, and later destinations.
- Character fates for every major and recurring character: death, disappearance,
  displacement, survival, or transformation.
- Record-preservation threads: archives, letters, objects, songs.
- Knowledge latency: rumours before effects; contradictory reports.
- Act II build-up and Act III breaking-point scenes.

**Hard rules:**

- No single cause. No villain responsible for the collapse.
- The player cannot prevent history wholesale; local outcomes remain changeable.
- Regional timing differs; do not synchronise events for drama.
- Every destroyed place has people who leave, not just ruins.

**Exit (Gate D part 1):** three regions experience the collapse differently; every
recurring character has a fate path; migration chains trace to later appearances; a
persona run reaches Act III without the main narrative blocking on luck.

## M16 — New Roads and the Long Memory (Acts IV–V)

**Goal:** rebuild, move on, and make the passage of generations legible.

**Window:** c. 1150–900 BCE, with time jumps.

**Deliverables:**

- New political and commercial arrangements: Neo-Hittite centres, Phoenician maritime
  networks where appropriate, Aramaean movements, Third Intermediate Egypt, Kushite
  consolidation at Napata, Neo-Assyrian rise, PGW/early South Asian developments, early
  Zhou.
- New communities founded by refugees; old destinations revisited and changed.
- Descendant generations for major families, with inherited stories, debts, objects, and
  grudges.
- Reworked route networks; some old routes never reopen.
- Local names for the protagonist become more divergent; stories about them mutate.
- Journal support for long gaps and explicit time jumps.
- Acts IV–V main threads: what the protagonist preserved, who they stayed with, and what
  they refused to become.

**Unique systems:** long-jump summaries that are authored, not auto-generated; descendant
recognition; story-variant tracking; changed-place revisits.

**Exit (Gate D part 2):** a 100-year jump is legible; descendants recognise the player
through authored bases; at least three old locations are revisited in changed form; no
character teleports across generations.

## M17 — Endgame and post-ending

**Goal:** endings composed from the journey, with no metaphysical verdict.

**Deliverables:**

- Ending composition from state: relationships, regions visited, languages, preserved
  knowledge, home or movement, faction ties, mystery stance.
- At least six reachable ending families: Keeper of Names, Friend of Peoples, King's
  Companion, Merchant Beyond the Horizon, The One Who Stayed, The Unremembered Traveler,
  plus Divine Name variations.
- Regional ending variations: Mediterranean-focused, Nile/southern, eastern, and
  far-eastern perspectives.
- Combined endings; no single final boss or mandatory destination.
- Post-ending travel that reflects the ending and lets remaining personal stories finish.
- Mystery payoff: encounters with apparent gods, contradictory interpretations, and a
  final choice about meaning — never confirmation.

**Exit (Gate E):** six endings reachable by different personas; no ending confirms or
denies divinity; post-ending state playable; all major arcs have a resolvable or
deliberately unresolved outcome recorded.

## M18 — Presentation, accessibility, and localisation

**Goal:** make the world memorable and the interface usable by more people.

**Art:**

- One unified art direction across all regions, phases, portraits, maps, and events. No
  mixed rendering styles, realism levels, or animation registers.
- Art bible per region: architecture, clothing, materials, landscape, pigments, and the
  shared style anchors from `docs/ASSETS.md`.
- Location illustrations with phase variants where the place changes.
- Character portraits with age, status, and travel variants.
- Map and route art; event illustrations; UI motifs from period materials.
- Commissioning briefs, asset IDs, placeholder system, review for caricature and style
  drift.

**Music and audio:**

- Regional instrument palettes and performance contexts from research.
- Diegetic music: courtyard songs, ritual chant, work songs, laments.
- A recurring protagonist theme that travels and mutates with cultures.
- Ambience for markets, harbours, workshops, rivers, weather, animals.
- Voice policy: subtitles mandatory if voice is used; no claims of authentic melody.

**UI/UX:**

- All screens from spec §18.1; readable at 150–200% text size.
- Explain why options are unavailable; compare prices and weight clearly.
- Journal, index, map, and codex support a cast and chronology this large.
- Accessibility: remapping, colour independence, reduced motion, dialogue log,
  save-anywhere or frequent autosave.

**Localisation:**

- Extraction, glossary, placeholder checks, text expansion tests.
- Cultural review of translated names and forms of address.

**Exit (Gate F part 1):** accessibility baseline met; all player text extracted; art and
audio pipelines documented; visual identity distinct per region.

## M19 — Release engineering

**Goal:** a stable, shippable build.

**Deliverables:**

- Tauri desktop packaging; Windows first, then platform decisions.
- Steam integration: build upload, cloud saves if supported, achievements if designed,
  store page assets, build branches.
- Save compatibility matrix across all public builds; migration dry runs.
- Performance and memory validation on target hardware.
- Crash reporting and privacy policy.
- Full QA pass: continuity, coverage, reachability, endings, localisation, accessibility.
- Legal and licensing review for art, music, fonts, and historical references.
- Release candidate and rollback plan.

**Exit (Gate F):** release candidate passes the full matrix; saves migrate from every
public build; no blocking continuity or accessibility defects.

## Re-planning rule

At the start of each wave, rewrite the relevant milestone files with:

- what the previous wave taught,
- updated content budgets,
- the actual destination list after research,
- risks discovered in playtests,
- any scope cut, with the reason.

The PLAN board is authoritative for order. This file is authoritative for intent. Never
let a stale milestone file override what the current STATUS and playtest evidence say.
